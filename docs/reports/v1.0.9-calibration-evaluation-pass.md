## Failure Analysis

### Repeated failure patterns

1. **versionOne is broken across 9 of 10 personas.** Every persona's First Buildable Version description used action verbs and concrete domain language ("Notion template", "CLI", "diagnostic engagement", "web app that connects to Gmail"). The `afterTranslations` table is tuned for emotional/state words (clear, confident, free, structured, capable, seen). Action descriptions trigger zero matches → fallback bullets fire → the most concrete section of the artifact becomes the most generic. Same fallback paragraph appears verbatim in P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6, P7, P8 (where versionOne is the user's whole product description), and P9. Only P10's versionOne ("Notion template + 30-day onboarding email course…") matched anything (and only marginally).

2. **Capability ↔ Reflection conflation.** Wiring `synthesizeReflection` to compose the Core Capability section means the section shows reflection-style framing, not capability statements. Worst examples: P2 ("The language you used points at something structural…"), P8 ("What you shared is real but is still in unstructured form…"), P10 (same emotional-dominant template). The Core Capability section should describe what the user *can do for someone else*, not meta-commentary on their input's structural shape.

3. **Builder erasure.** Personas with clear domain-specific input (P1 backend tooling, P3 brand design, P8 SaaS product) get the least useful output. The capability/idealUser/versionOne sections all flatten into self-help-style framing. The translation tables don't recognize builder vocabulary (shipped, MRR, customer, churn, scope, prototype, validation, pilot, integration). The system's "intelligence" is calibrated for one register only.

4. **Domain-specialist erasure.** P9 (wellness practitioner) gets actively translated AWAY from her own professional vocabulary. Her clients' "scattered + anxious + safe space" experience IS the product she sells; the system reframes it as a productivity-system gap. Same risk would apply to therapists, coaches, somatic practitioners, contemplative teachers.

5. **idealUser fallback fires for 4 of 10 personas (P1, P5, P6, P7).** When the ideal-user description doesn't contain emotional-state keywords, the section becomes generic. P5 wrote a rich, specific description of a startup founder ("founder of a 15-50 person startup whose team is in conflict and she doesn't know whether the issue is people, process, or strategy") — and got the 3-bullet fallback because the regex didn't match.

6. **System never adds actionable content to pathForward.** PathForward is intentionally raw (v1.0.8 decision). For every persona, the only specific actions in the artifact are the ones the user typed. The system contributes zero forward-momentum content.

## Calibration Read

### How intelligent is the software currently?
**Modest, narrow, honest about its narrow range when it lands.** It has one piece of real intelligence: catching ~50 specific emotional/state phrases and translating them into structural framing. That intelligence is genuinely useful for a specific user — the capable-but-unclear nontechnical builder per the founding doctrine. For users outside that profile, the system is roughly equivalent to a templated form that fills sections with structurally-correct boilerplate.

### Where is it properly calibrated?
- **Aspirationally-confused users** with self-help-vocabulary inputs (P2, P4, P5, P6, P7). The reflection's emotional-dominant branch and the fallback structuralization correctly diagnose the user's state and tell them what they need.
- **Layer-mismatch detection** (P6, P7). When the user is genuinely at the wrong layer (identity-domain, aspirational, weak input), the system honestly names that — and the prescribed next move ("name one specific person…") is correct.
- **The doctrinal first-user.** A capable-but-unclear nontechnical person who wrote about feeling lost would be served well; this is exactly the founding doctrine's user. Score for that profile: ~4.0.

### Where is it improperly calibrated?
- **Confident builders with clear domain content** (P1, P3, P8). The system has no vocabulary for shipped products, paying customers, scope decisions, technical specifics. It treats their rich content as undifferentiated text.
- **Domain specialists with non-self-help vocabulary** (P9). The system translates their professional language INTO self-help language, contrary to their practice.
- **Action/scope content** (everywhere versionOne and pathForward). The system has no translator for "do X this week, then Y, then Z" content.
- **The Capability section.** Wired to the wrong synthesizer. It currently echoes Reflection-style meta-commentary instead of describing capability.

### Is it more reflective than transformative?
**Strongly more reflective.** The system reframes input; it does not generate new understanding from rich input. When the user already understands themselves (P1, P8), the system has nothing to offer. The "transformation" is a structural-vocabulary substitution, not a content-level synthesis. Real transformation would require the system to *find new patterns the user did not notice*, not just translate words they already used.

### Does it generalize across users?
**No.** Per-persona scores range from 2.0 to 4.25 — a 2.25-point spread on a 5-point scale. The system is finely tuned to ~30% of the persona space and inappropriate for ~30%, with mixed results in the middle.

## Recommended Next Moves (ranked)

### #1 — Decouple Core Capability from Reflection synthesis
**Why highest leverage**: Capability is the first blueprint section the user reads. It currently shows wrong-category content (reflection-style meta-commentary about their input's structural shape) for 4–5 of 10 personas. A capability synthesizer should aggregate signals from the 5 capability answers (not the seed) and produce *capability statements* in the form "You can reliably help [user type] [do something]" — concrete, externally-observable, not meta. Single-file change to `page.tsx`'s `formatCapability` plus a new `synthesizeCapability` helper. Highest impact per unit work.

### #2 — Build a `versionOne` action-translator
**Why second-highest**: versionOne fails 9 of 10 personas with the same generic fallback paragraph. The fix is a translator tuned for action+scope language: detect "[noun] that does [verb]" patterns, scope-limiting language ("smallest", "first", "no backend"), platform/tool/service nouns, and produce structural bullets like *"Version one is a [noun-form] that performs [verb] for [user-type]"*. Without this, the most concrete section of the blueprint stays the most generic.

### #3 — Expand vocabulary across builder + domain registers
**Why third**: The translation tables are heavily skewed toward self-help vocabulary. Adding ~30–50 entries for builder/SaaS/founder vocabulary (shipped, MRR, customer, churn, scope, prototype, validation, pilot, integration, retention) and ~20 entries for wellness/somatic vocabulary (felt sense, nervous system, embodiment, presence, regulation) would close the worst miscalibration gaps without changing architecture. Should also pair with a "domain-aware preservation rule" so professional vocabulary in P9-style inputs is *kept structural* rather than translated AWAY from the practitioner's register.

### Honorable mentions (not in top 3 but worth noting)

- **idealUser translator gap**: ideal-user descriptions often contain user-archetype text ("a founder of a 15-50 person startup") that doesn't match before-state regex. A translator for "user-archetype + struggle pattern" would catch P5-style content.
- **System-contribution to pathForward**: currently zero. A path-forward synthesizer that augments user-typed actions with one structural recommendation per bucket would close the actionability gap.
- **Vocabulary preservation rule for professional domain language**: detect when input is *describing a domain practice* vs *describing personal struggle*, and route differently.

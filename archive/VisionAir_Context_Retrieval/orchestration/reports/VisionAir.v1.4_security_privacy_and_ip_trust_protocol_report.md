## 3. Trust Model — Summary

The VisionAir trust model rests on four interlocking commitments:

- **Custodianship, not ownership.** VisionAir stores and helps structure user-created business creation context. It does not become the creative owner. Ownership remains with the user across raw seeds, structured regions, branches (active and collapsed), blueprints, syntheses, and Guided Continuation outputs the user accepts.
- **Private by default, in every direction.** Projects are private from other projects (cross-project isolation), from other users (no default user-to-user exposure), from broad system surfaces (logs, analytics, observability), and from the public (no public-by-URL surfaces). Relaxations are explicit, justified, opt-in, granular, and revocable — never the default of convenience.
- **AI assistance does not weaken trust.** AI processing operates inside the platform's trust boundary. It cannot silently overwrite confirmed truth, cannot strip ownership, cannot lower confidentiality, and cannot escape the protocol's expectations by virtue of being model-driven.
- **Trust is real, not cosmetic.** Deletion is real (reaches derived summaries, caches, branch history, backups). Export is high-fidelity (preserves truth-status labels and provenance). Portability is real (the user can leave with what they brought plus what they built). These commitments precede legal obligations.

These four commitments collectively form the trust contract VisionAir signs with every user the moment they plant a seed.

## 5. User Ownership / IP Expectations — Confirmed Formalized

VisionAir.v1.4 §5 formalizes user ownership and IP expectations as the single most important commitment in the trust protocol:

- **users retain ownership** of the ideas and project content they create in VisionAir
- **VisionAir is a custodian, not a co-author**, regardless of how much structuring assistance it provided
- **AI assistance does not constitute platform authorship**, even when a system suggestion materially shaped the project
- **derived outputs remain the user's** — blueprints, syntheses, accepted suggestions, branches, and ranking decisions all inherit user ownership
- **rejected branches remain the user's IP** — what the user explored and chose not to pursue is still their thinking
- **future implementation must not blur assistance with ownership transfer** — interfaces, terms of service, and backend behavior are bound to this expectation

This satisfies the prompt's requirement that user ownership / IP expectations be formalized as the baseline principle for user-generated business creation content.

## 6. Privacy, Confidentiality, AI-Processing Boundaries, Export/Deletion — Confirmed Formalized

Four trust commitments are now formally established at the protocol level:

### 6.1 Privacy (§6)
Private-by-default. No analytics-substrate use. Privacy survives sessions and devices. Equal privacy across all maturity levels. Real deletion as a real expectation.

### 6.2 Confidentiality (§7)
Need-to-process only. Minimum necessary visibility. No cross-project leakage. No default user-to-user exposure. No broad-surface copies of project content. Logs are not a confidentiality loophole. AI assistance is not a confidentiality discount.

### 6.3 AI-Processing Boundaries (§8)
Four state categories distinguished. AI processing operates inside the platform's trust boundary. No silent override of confirmed truth. AI-generated content inherits project sensitivity. Assistance vs authorship stays visibly separate. Project content sent for AI processing remains inside the platform's security expectations — including the rejection of integrations whose terms cannot be aligned with this protocol.

### 6.4 Export / Deletion (§11)
Export as a user right; meaningful representation including provenance and labels. Export fidelity matters. Portability matters because projects contain original IP. Deletion is real and reaches the entire trail (derived summaries, caches, branch history, suggestion history, backups within a transparent window). Trust expectations precede legal obligations.

This satisfies the prompt's requirement that privacy, confidentiality, AI-processing boundaries, and export/deletion expectations be formalized.
